3. Understanding the metrics

  • Precision: Of the rows flagged, how many were truly anomalous.
  • Recall: Of the labelled anomalies, how many were detected.
  • F1: Combines precision and recall into one score.
  • Average precision: Summarizes the precision-recall curve and is particularly informative when anomalies are rare.
  • ROC-AUC: Measures how well scores rank positives above negatives over different thresholds.
  • False-positive rate: The fraction of normal rows incorrectly flagged as anomalous.

6. Limitations and next steps

  • Verify that the test set is independent of training and validation data.
  • Choose thresholds on validation data rather than tuning on final test labels.
  • Inspect false positives and missed anomalies by host, time, and scenario if those fields are available in the original predictions.
  • Confirm whether sequence and row-based models score the same test population.
  • For supervised models, check whether training anomalies resemble real deployment anomalies.
  • Save the chosen model, preprocessing configuration, feature list, and threshold together for reproducibility.
